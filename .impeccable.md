## Design Context

### Users

The primary reader is an informed non-specialist: a curious adult who wants to understand the evidence behind social media's public costs without needing training in economics, epidemiology, or causal inference. The main jobs are to test assumptions, inspect primary sources, understand uncertainty, share a defensible scenario, and return when the evidence changes.

### Brand Personality

Serious, humane, and exact. The site should earn confidence through clear language, visible sources, honest limits, and calm interaction. It should not use fear, spectacle, sales pressure, or false certainty.

### Aesthetic Direction

Editorial evidence-first. Treat the calculator like an interactive feature in a rigorous magazine: strong typography, restrained color, generous reading space, compact data controls, and source notes placed close to claims. Use a light, paper-like theme with ink, muted rules, and a single harm-red accent. Avoid dashboard chrome, emoji labels, generic cards, gradients, glass effects, and decorative metrics.

### Design Principles

1. Put the calculator and its current result before supporting material.
2. Keep evidence and uncertainty beside every consequential assumption.
3. Use one clear action at a time: adjust, inspect, copy, or share.
4. Identity appears only in the explicit, consented research-pack form. It never enters analytics, replay, calculator state, or error context.
5. Make the main journey work by keyboard and at a 390px viewport.
6. Prefer durable static HTML and repository-owned CSS over runtime styling services.
