A natural experiment

What happened to teenagers
in 2012?

The share of U.S. adolescents who had a major depressive episode in the past year was roughly flat from 2004 to 2011. Then it began rising. It hasn't stopped.

It is not only the United States.

The U.K., Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and the Nordic countries show the same inflection. Same direction. Same timing. Same magnitude. Every English-speaking democracy that adopted smartphone-based adolescence in that window saw teenage mental health turn at the same point.

Six countries do not turn in lockstep by accident. Something common to all of them changed in those years. There is one obvious candidate.

A correlation.

A causal claim.

Many things rose in the 2010s. Smartphone adoption. Algorithmic feeds. Sleep loss. Helicopter parenting. Political polarization. Climate anxiety. A striking chart is not the same as proof.

The strongest causal evidence in this literature is narrower and quieter than the chart above.

  • 01 Allcott, Braghieri, Eichmeyer, and Gentzkow (2020). Randomly paid 2,743 U.S. adults to deactivate Facebook for four weeks. Measured improvements in subjective well-being. A real experiment, not a correlation.
  • 02 Haugen (2021), the Facebook Files. Meta's own internal research, leaked: Instagram knew it made body image worse for one in three teen girls. Internal causal language. Internal data.
  • 03 Twenge & Haidt (2024). Cross-country replication: the same generational inflection across the English-speaking world plus the Nordics. Coordinated timing rules out most local explanations.

The question we cannot run.

What would the curve above look like if smartphone-based adolescence had not happened? If Instagram had shipped with the age gates Meta's own researchers recommended? If TikTok's recommender weighted differently? If schools had banned phones in 2014?

These are counterfactuals. You cannot randomize a generation. The human-subjects experiment that would answer the question is the one we are not allowed to run. The one already running, on every adolescent alive, is the one we cannot undo.

Subconscious builds AI agents that simulate populations under conditions that did not occur. Counterfactual evidence at the scale of real markets. The same machinery that lets a brand test a campaign before launch lets a policymaker test an intervention before passing it. We're applying it to the questions human-subjects research will never run.

A legal record, March to May 2026

The courts are starting to name it.

A page like this used to live in the academic frame. Then twelve jurors started putting numbers on the externality. In ten weeks: a $375M verdict in New Mexico, a negligent-design verdict in California, a state supreme court greenlighting Massachusetts, and Meta's first settlement with a school district.

  1. Mar 24, 2026  ·  New Mexico
    Jury orders Meta to pay $375M for failing to protect young users from predators on Facebook and Instagram. State AG case under consumer-protection statute. NPR
  2. Mar 25, 2026  ·  California
    Los Angeles jury finds Meta and Google negligent in design, awards $6M ($3M compensatory, $3M punitive). First bellwether verdict in California's consolidated docket of ~1,600 plaintiffs. Meta on the hook for 70%. PBS
  3. Apr 10, 2026  ·  Massachusetts
    SJC rules Meta must face the AG's youth-addiction lawsuit. One of 40+ state AG cases now active. CNBC
  4. May 21, 2026  ·  Kentucky
    Meta settles the first U.S. school-district case. School districts had argued the platforms drove a mental-health crisis they were forced to absorb the cost of. Terms undisclosed. CNBC

The federal MDL has 2,400+ active cases. The CA consolidated docket has ~1,600 plaintiffs. The externality the page above argues for is the externality a jury just put a dollar amount on a piece of.
